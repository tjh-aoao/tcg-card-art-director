# TCG 大厂卡图生产系统蒸馏

This reference converts public, transferable production patterns into a Zone War art workflow. Use it for method, not imitation.

Sources used for the distilled model:

- Wizards of the Coast describes a pipeline split into Vision Design, Set Design, and Play Design, with world/theme planning preceding final card execution and art lock-in creating downstream constraints: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/vision-design-set-design-and-play-design-2017-10-23
- Konami's Yu-Gi-Oh! TCG maintains a Problem-Solving Card Text hub, showing the value of controlled language for consistent interpretation: https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/play/psct/
- The Pokemon TCG Illustration Contest guidelines show production constraints around recognizable subject, world fit, composition, frame/template safety, file specs, originality, and review by directors/judges: https://www.ptcgic-cr.com/2024/en/guidelines/

## 女娲式提炼: How They Think

### 1. Product-first art

Large TCG art serves a product matrix: set identity, card type, rarity, gameplay role, brand fit, print readability, and player desire. The image is not judged only as illustration; it is judged as a component in a collectible, playable, expandable system.

Transfer to Zone War:
- Every card art brief starts with card type, gameplay role, attribute, rarity, and set function.
- Illustration quality is insufficient if the card cannot be recognized quickly in hand, on table, and in export thumbnails.

### 2. Vision before execution

Big card games separate early vision from later execution. Early phases define world, themes, mechanical identity, and art constraints. Later phases produce individual cards and refine them against play, product, and production limits.

Transfer to Zone War:
- Series Bible before Single Card Brief.
- Do not commission final art for cards below Candidate status unless it is prototype/placeholder art.
- If rules or card role changes, re-check whether the art still represents the card.

### 3. Controlled language and controlled visuals

Yu-Gi-Oh!'s text discipline has an art-direction equivalent: reusable visual grammar. The goal is not identical images, but predictable interpretation.

Transfer to Zone War:
- Each faction/series needs controlled motifs, color logic, material logic, silhouette logic, and prohibited motifs.
- Repeat visual grammar for attribute, card type, and gameplay role.

### 4. Subject legibility

Pokemon's official illustration guidance emphasizes recognizable main subject, appropriate scenario, world fit, template awareness, originality, and print-quality constraints.

Transfer to Zone War:
- Make the card's main subject unmistakable.
- Choose a scenario that shows how the subject behaves, not a generic pose.
- Keep important information out of card-frame conflict zones.
- Avoid real-world text, logos, celebrity likeness, and third-party IP unless explicitly licensed.

### 5. Art lock-in risk

Once art is assigned, late card changes become expensive. The art may imply movement, flight, scale, faction, weapon, or rules identity that the final card no longer has.

Transfer to Zone War:
- Assign final art only after the card's role and text are stable.
- Use low-cost concept art during Draft/Test.
- Every art brief should include "rule-lock assumptions": what cannot change without revising art.

## Production Pipeline

1. Series Vision
   - Define theme promise, audience emotion, mechanical identity, visual pillars, prohibited references, and rarity ladder.

2. Card Role Mapping
   - Map each card to gameplay role, table readability, attribute/faction signals, and emotional beat.

3. Art Brief
   - Specify subject, action, composition, camera, palette, materials, lighting, background, card-frame safety, and forbidden elements.

4. Concept Pass
   - Generate or request 2-4 thumbnails with different silhouettes/compositions.

5. Art Director Review
   - Check role clarity, world fit, frame safety, uniqueness, print readability, and style consistency.

6. Final Prompt / Commission Brief
   - Convert approved direction into AI prompt and artist brief.

7. Post-art QA
   - Check export crop, card-frame overlap, thumbnail recognition, grayscale readability, and consistency with neighboring cards.

## Anti-patterns

- Art before card role.
- Generic "epic fantasy" prompts without gameplay identity.
- Every rare card using the same glow, angle, and palette.
- Overloaded backgrounds that hide the subject.
- World drift: modern signs, recognizable real buildings, imported symbols, or visual jokes outside the setting.
- AI prompt as the only brief, leaving no art director criteria.
